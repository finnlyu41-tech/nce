## Natural variant policy

The answer set deliberately includes contractions such as `she'll`, `won't`, `we'd better`, and `it's`; ordinary fronted time phrases; equivalent clock readings; and `in + period` with or without possessive `time`. All questions retain one final question mark. Case, whitespace, apostrophe shape, and permitted omission of a final statement full stop are already covered by the supplied scope and are not multiplied into separate variants.

Each n93 correction has bounded alternatives for repeating both subjects/times, omitting the second subject while retaining affirmative `will`, putting one shared time first, and joining two complete clauses with a semicolon. A semicolon yields one sentence. A repeated subject is optional when coordination clearly shares it; repeated time is optional when the common time clearly governs both clauses. The variants never replace a correction with only the correct destination, and never divide it into two full-stop-separated sentences. Ellipsis that removes the explicitly required affirmative `will` entirely was excluded.

Natural general-English substitutes such as `should`, `call me`, `have a day off`, or `hurry up` were conservatively excluded when they replace or extend the specified lexical phrase. They can be valid English while falling outside this bounded drill. Present-continuous travel plans were also not supplied as variants for the visible past/future drill, which explicitly guides the learner toward past forms or `will`.

## Meaning and near-wrong boundaries

Past records require `flew`, `went`, or `returned`, and past questions require `did` plus the base verb. `Has flown ... ago`, future statements for completed events, and `did ... went` do not preserve the requested tense or grammar. `The month/year after next` differs from `next month/year`; `in a month` is not interchangeable with a calendar `next month`. `In twenty minutes` is a stated delay, `within twenty minutes` is a deadline range, and `for twenty minutes` is a duration.

Direction and person are consequential. `Arrive from Madrid` identifies a departure city, while `arrive in Madrid` identifies an arrival city. `Returned to Tokyo` cannot become `returned from Tokyo`. `My car`, `his bags`, `telephone me`, and `wait for him` preserve specific ownership or participant relations. `Had better` requires a bare infinitive; `be careful` needs `be`, and `wait for him` needs `for`.

The clock answers are determinate: a slow clock displaying 08:15 gives actual **08:25** by adding ten minutes; a fast clock displaying 07:20 gives actual **07:15** by subtracting five minutes. The reliable departure is **09:17**, and the reliable current reading is **11:40**. English digital readings and their past/to equivalents are valid variants. `What time` specifically requests a clock time; `When` can include a date and does not preserve the prescribed question phrase.
